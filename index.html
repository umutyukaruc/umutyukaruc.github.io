About Me

My work primarily sits at the intersection of critical security studies, populism, conspiracy theories, foreign policy and popular culture.

Currently, I am conducting research as a Visiting Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Tübingen, Germany, while maintaining my role as a Faculty Member at Nevşehir Hacı Bektaş Veli University in Turkey.

My academic journey is driven by a deep curiosity about how emotions, discourse, and non-traditional security threats shape domestic and international political landscapes. I strive to bridge theoretical insights with empirical analyses to understand the complexities of modern populism and foreign policy making.

Featured Project

Affective Conspiracies

TÜBİTAK 2219 Postdoctoral Research Project

This ongoing research project examines how populist actors in Germany and Türkiye use conspiracy-laden migration narratives to mobilize emotions such as fear and anger and to frame migration as an existential security threat. Focusing comparatively on the political parties, the project combines securitization theory, populism studies, conspiracy theory research, and the study of political emotions through intertextual discourse analysis of speeches, party documents, social media, and visual-cultural material. It aims to address the limited scholarship on the emotional mechanisms through which conspiracy narratives contribute to the securitization of migration across different political and cultural contexts.

Status: Ongoing at University of Tübingen
